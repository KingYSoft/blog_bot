## Method and scope

The briefing date follows the Asia/Shanghai calendar. GitHub candidates came from the official [GitHub Trending weekly page][gh-weekly]. Lifetime stars, descriptions, languages, and last-push times were checked against the [GitHub REST API][gh-api] and repository READMEs. Ranking weighs weekly placement and star velocity, current development activity, usefulness, and topic diversity—not lifetime stars alone. “Stars this week” is GitHub’s displayed point-in-time figure and can change during the day.

News discovery covered August 5–7, prioritizing events on August 6–7. Event dates were checked separately from publication timestamps. Corporate or market numbers were compared with AP, official filings, company releases, legislation, or regulator material where available. Reuters links may encounter regional routing or bot protection; no figure was inferred from a headline alone.

## 15 startup experiments for a solo software founder

These ideas are filtered for a software-first MVP, low initial spend, internet distribution, and one-person execution. They are ranked roughly by solo feasibility, willingness to pay, acquisition difficulty, and leverage from software-development skill.

### 1. Open-model commercial-use meter

- **Problem:** Teams deploy open-weight models without a durable record of license versions, revenue thresholds, hosted providers, or downstream obligations.
- **Solution:** A CI and runtime service that inventories models, snapshots licenses, maps deployments to commercial-use triggers, and creates renewal alerts.
- **Narrow customer:** 5–50 person AI SaaS companies using three or more open models.
- **Solo leverage:** Package scanners, model-registry APIs, policy-as-code, and GitHub checks make the product automation-heavy and support-light.
- **Business model:** $49–$199 monthly by repositories and deployed models.
- **1–2 week MVP:** Scan Hugging Face/GitHub model references from one repository, diff five common licenses, and emit a human-review checklist in CI.
- **First-user channel:** Maintainer forums, MLOps Slack groups, and direct outreach to startups announcing open-model stacks.
- **First paid validation:** Three teams pay at least $49 for a live license-change alert and exported evidence report.
- **Main risk:** It must clearly remain workflow assistance, not legal advice, and license interpretation needs careful sourcing.

### 2. Agent-output accessibility linter

- **Problem:** Internal agents bury actions in long prose, creating avoidable cognitive load for users with ADHD, dyslexia, limited language fluency, or time pressure.
- **Solution:** An API/proxy that scores answer-first structure, step clarity, reading load, missing completion criteria, and filler before responses reach users.
- **Narrow customer:** Teams building support or coding copilots for technical users.
- **Solo leverage:** Deterministic text checks plus small-model rewrites can provide value without training a model.
- **Business model:** Usage-based API with a $39/month team dashboard.
- **1–2 week MVP:** Ship ten rules, a browser playground, and middleware for one OpenAI-compatible endpoint; compare 100 before/after answers.
- **First-user channel:** Accessibility communities, indie-hacker groups, and open-source agent maintainers.
- **First paid validation:** One team pays and keeps the linter enabled on at least 1,000 production responses in 30 days.
- **Main risk:** Over-compression can remove nuance; users need selectable profiles rather than one universal style.

### 3. Local-LLM hardware fit calculator

- **Problem:** Developers cannot reliably tell whether a model, quantization, context window, and concurrency target will fit their Mac, GPU workstation, or older server.
- **Solution:** A calculator that turns model architecture and hardware inventory into RAM/VRAM, SSD-streaming, throughput, power, and cost scenarios.
- **Narrow customer:** Independent AI developers and small agencies buying or repurposing $2,000–$20,000 hardware.
- **Solo leverage:** Public model configs and benchmark submissions support a useful rules engine before a large dataset exists.
- **Business model:** Free calculator; $29 project reports and $99/month team inventory.
- **1–2 week MVP:** Support AirLLM, ds4, llama.cpp, and ten popular models across Apple Silicon and six GPU classes.
- **First-user channel:** LocalLLaMA communities, hardware forums, model Discords, and SEO pages for model–GPU pairs.
- **First paid validation:** Ten paid reports or one team subscription before adding more runtimes.
- **Main risk:** Benchmarks vary with kernels and settings; outputs must show confidence ranges and community-supplied provenance.

### 4. Agent-memory privacy scrubber

- **Problem:** Shared agent memory quietly accumulates secrets, personal data, stale instructions, and content users later cannot locate or delete.
- **Solution:** A proxy that classifies proposed memory writes, blocks sensitive fields, attaches source/expiry metadata, and verifies deletion across stores.
- **Narrow customer:** Software teams running two or more agents with a shared vector or graph memory.
- **Solo leverage:** A gateway architecture can integrate existing stores without replacing them.
- **Business model:** $79/month per workspace plus self-hosted license.
- **1–2 week MVP:** Connect one vector database and one agent SDK; demonstrate write policies, expiry, search, and signed deletion receipts.
- **First-user channel:** GitHub issues for memory frameworks, agent-builder Discords, and targeted demos to small consultancies.
- **First paid validation:** Two teams pay to scrub a real memory corpus and keep the gateway installed.
- **Main risk:** False negatives can expose data; conservative defaults and local deployment are essential.

### 5. Signed agent-work replay for GitHub

- **Problem:** A pull request shows the final diff but not which prompts, tool calls, approvals, or external data caused an agent to change code.
- **Solution:** A GitHub App that records a signed, redacted event timeline and attaches a replayable provenance summary to each agent-created PR.
- **Narrow customer:** Regulated fintech and security teams piloting coding agents on private repositories.
- **Solo leverage:** GitHub webhooks, attestations, and existing agent logs avoid building a new collaboration suite.
- **Business model:** $15 per developer monthly; higher-priced self-hosted archive.
- **1–2 week MVP:** Support one agent CLI and GitHub PRs; capture commands, diffs, approvals, and redaction events in an immutable manifest.
- **First-user channel:** Security engineering newsletters, GitHub Marketplace, and compliance-focused developer communities.
- **First paid validation:** One five-seat team pays after using replay evidence in an actual code review.
- **Main risk:** Logs themselves may contain secrets, so local redaction and customer-controlled retention must precede upload.

### 6. Private-fund plain-language comparison sheet

- **Problem:** Retail investors see private-market funds inside fintech apps but struggle to compare fees, liquidity gates, valuation frequency, concentration, and conflicts.
- **Solution:** An educational parser that converts official offering documents into side-by-side, citation-backed risk sheets without recommendations or rankings.
- **Narrow customer:** Fee-only financial educators and small registered advisers reviewing fintech-distributed alternative funds.
- **Solo leverage:** Document extraction, schema validation, and citation mapping are tractable software tasks.
- **Business model:** $39 per document or $99/month professional workspace.
- **1–2 week MVP:** Parse five public fund documents into a fixed 20-field schema and allow reviewers to correct every extracted value.
- **First-user channel:** Adviser newsletters, financial-planning communities, and SEO around exact fund names.
- **First paid validation:** Five professionals pay for a cited comparison and at least three reuse the tool.
- **Main risk:** This must remain educational document processing, not suitability or investment advice; extraction errors require visible source citations.

### 7. AI-therapy marketing and workflow preflight

- **Problem:** Wellness-app founders accidentally use clinical claims or autonomous flows that cross emerging therapy rules.
- **Solution:** A pre-release checker for app copy and flow diagrams that flags restricted claims, missing consent, absent human review, and crisis-escalation gaps.
- **Narrow customer:** U.S. consumer wellness chatbot teams with fewer than 20 employees.
- **Solo leverage:** State bill text can be translated into a transparent rules library with dated citations.
- **Business model:** $79 per release review plus $39/month rule updates.
- **1–2 week MVP:** Cover California SB 903 concepts, scan landing-page text and one JSON flow, and generate an issue list for counsel review.
- **First-user channel:** Digital-health accelerators, founder groups, and direct outreach to app-store wellness launches.
- **First paid validation:** Two founders pay before launch and take at least one flagged item to counsel.
- **Main risk:** Regulations change and legal interpretation is sensitive; the product must explicitly support—not replace—licensed legal and clinical review.

### 8. Youth-safety evidence pack generator

- **Problem:** Small social products lack a repeatable way to prove age-gate behavior, privacy defaults, recommendation limits, and remediation tests.
- **Solution:** A test harness that runs scripted minor-account journeys and exports timestamped screenshots, settings, network events, and failed assertions.
- **Narrow customer:** Social, gaming, and creator apps with 10,000–500,000 monthly users.
- **Solo leverage:** Browser automation and evidence packaging can deliver value without becoming an identity-verification vendor.
- **Business model:** $99 per test run or $249/month continuous checks.
- **1–2 week MVP:** Test registration, default visibility, DM permissions, and deletion on one demo app using synthetic accounts.
- **First-user channel:** Trust-and-safety communities, privacy consultants, and app-development agencies.
- **First paid validation:** One customer buys a report used in a board, insurer, or counsel review.
- **Main risk:** Synthetic journeys do not prove full compliance; the report must state coverage and limitations precisely.

### 9. AI-capex pressure dashboard for small suppliers

- **Problem:** Contractors serving data centers cannot connect public announcements to local demand for power, cooling, construction, and specialized labor.
- **Solution:** A location-based feed that converts capex releases and permit data into dated demand signals and customer-specific watchlists.
- **Narrow customer:** Independent electrical, cooling, and network contractors in one U.S. state or one Indian metro.
- **Solo leverage:** Public permits, company releases, and maps support a narrow data product with no enterprise integrations.
- **Business model:** $49/month local watchlist or sponsored regional report.
- **1–2 week MVP:** Cover one region, track 30 facilities, and deliver weekly alerts with source links and confidence labels.
- **First-user channel:** Local trade associations, LinkedIn outreach, and regional contractor newsletters.
- **First paid validation:** Five contractors pay for one month and two request alerts for named projects.
- **Main risk:** Announced capacity may be delayed or canceled; signals must distinguish plans, permits, construction, and operation.

### 10. Cross-border wealth onboarding checklist builder

- **Problem:** Small wealth teams repeatedly assemble country-, product-, and client-specific document lists by hand, causing delays and missing disclosures.
- **Solution:** A rules-based intake portal that generates a cited checklist, requests documents, and tracks expiry without giving tax or investment advice.
- **Narrow customer:** Independent advisers and family-office administrators onboarding India–Singapore or India–GIFT City clients.
- **Solo leverage:** Narrow jurisdiction scope and configurable rules make a useful MVP possible without core-banking integration.
- **Business model:** $25 per case or $149/month for a small practice.

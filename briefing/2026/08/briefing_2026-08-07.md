## Method and scope

The briefing date follows the Asia/Shanghai calendar. GitHub candidates came from the official [GitHub Trending weekly page][gh-weekly]. Lifetime stars, descriptions, languages, and last-push times were checked against the [GitHub REST API][gh-api] and repository READMEs. Ranking weighs weekly placement and star velocity, current development activity, usefulness, and topic diversity—not lifetime stars alone. “Stars this week” is GitHub’s displayed point-in-time figure and can change during the day.

News discovery covered August 5–7, prioritizing events on August 6–7. Event dates were checked separately from publication timestamps. Corporate or market numbers were compared with AP, official filings, company releases, legislation, or regulator material where available. Reuters links may encounter regional routing or bot protection; no figure was inferred from a headline alone.

## 15 startup experiments for a solo software founder

The candidate pool was built by turning the 10 repositories and 20 news events into concrete workflow problems, then adding adjacent stable needs observable in existing paid software or manual professional work. Overlapping concepts were merged and every candidate was screened against one-person delivery, a 1–2 week MVP, a sub-$500 launch budget, direct internet distribution, regulatory risk, and a real payment test. The 15 survivors below are distinct experiments, ranked by **solo feasibility + willingness to pay + acquisition ease + developer-skill fit**; each dimension is scored 1–5.

### 1. Open-model commercial-use meter

- **Positioning:** “Dependabot for open-model commercial terms” for small AI SaaS teams.
- **Pain and evidence:** Teams deploy open weights without durable records of license versions, revenue thresholds, providers, or downstream duties. Alibaba’s reported move toward charging large commercial users shows that access terms can become a live operating cost, not a one-time legal read [Reuters][t1].
- **Target and first 20 prospects:** Engineering founders or MLOps leads at 20 identifiable, 5–50 person AI SaaS firms that publicly list at least three Hugging Face models in repositories, model cards, or job posts.
- **Product and difference:** Inventory models, snapshot source terms, map deployments to commercial triggers, and alert on changes. Unlike a generic software-composition scanner, it understands model cards, gated downloads, inference providers, and model-specific revenue/use clauses; output remains a counsel-review checklist, not legal advice.
- **Solo leverage:** Package scanners, model-registry APIs, policy-as-code, and GitHub checks make the product automation-heavy and support-light.
- **MVP (1–2 weeks):** (1) scan one GitHub repository, (2) resolve Hugging Face/GitHub model references, (3) snapshot and diff five common license/terms pages, and (4) post a cited CI checklist. **Do not build:** automated legal conclusions, enterprise SSO, or more than one source-control integration.
- **Implementation:** Next.js, Postgres, GitHub App/webhooks, Hugging Face Hub API, Playwright snapshots, and Open Policy Agent/Rego rules.
- **Revenue and test price:** $49/month for five models; $149/month for 25 models and evidence export.
- **Acquisition and first outreach:** Search GitHub and launch posts for public model dependencies; email the founder a free one-page inventory: “I found four model terms in your public stack—want the dated diff report before your next deploy?”
- **7-day validation:** Days 1–2 interview five leads and manually audit five stacks; day 3 publish a sample diff; days 4–5 send 30 personalized audits; days 6–7 run three live repositories and ask for payment before adding coverage.
- **First paid gate:** Three real teams pay at least $49 for a live alert plus evidence export; trials and waitlist signups do not count.
- **Risk, falsification, and stop:** Legal ambiguity and false assurance are the main risks. Fastest disproof: prospects say annual counsel review is sufficient and will not pay for monitoring. Stop after 30 qualified contacts and five demos if there are no two paid pilots or five conditional purchase commitments.
- **Score:** feasibility **5/5** · willingness to pay **5/5** · acquisition ease **4/5** · developer fit **5/5** · **total 19/20**.

### 2. Agent-output accessibility linter

- **Positioning:** A CI-style quality gate that makes support and coding-agent output easier to scan and act on.
- **Pain and evidence:** Internal agents bury actions in long prose, increasing cognitive load for users with ADHD, dyslexia, limited fluency, or time pressure. The deliberately narrow [i-have-adhd](https://github.com/ayghri/i-have-adhd) skill gained 3,628 weekly stars, strong demand evidence for action-first formatting; willingness to pay for a production gate is still **to be validated**.
- **Target and first 20 prospects:** Product/engineering leads at 20 B2B SaaS companies with an in-product support or coding copilot and fewer than 100 employees.
- **Product and difference:** Score answer-first structure, step clarity, reading load, missing completion criteria, and filler before delivery. Unlike a general grammar checker, profiles are task- and accessibility-specific and produce regression metrics across releases.
- **Solo leverage:** Deterministic text checks plus small-model rewrites can provide value without training a model.
- **MVP (1–2 weeks):** (1) ten deterministic rules, (2) three selectable profiles, (3) OpenAI-compatible proxy middleware, and (4) a 100-response before/after report. **Do not build:** a new chatbot, automatic rewriting by default, or full WCAG certification.
- **Implementation:** TypeScript/Fastify, unified/remark AST, textstat-style readability metrics, optional small-model rewrite, and OpenTelemetry events.
- **Revenue and test price:** $39/month for 10,000 scored responses; $99/month with team regressions.
- **Acquisition and first outreach:** Find SaaS copilots in Product Hunt and GitHub; send the lead a redacted ten-response audit: “Three recurring patterns hide the next action—may I test your preferred profile on a staging endpoint?”
- **7-day validation:** Days 1–2 interview five builders and two accessibility practitioners; day 3 score 100 public examples; day 4 publish the rubric; days 5–6 send 25 audits; day 7 request a paid staging install.
- **First paid gate:** Two teams each pay at least $39 and one keeps it enabled for 1,000 real responses; free API calls do not count.
- **Risk, falsification, and stop:** Compression may remove nuance or teams may see this as a prompt tweak. Fastest disproof: no measurable preference in blind tests. Stop if 100 paired ratings show under a 15-point preference lift and no paid pilot after 25 qualified outreaches.
- **Score:** feasibility **5/5** · willingness to pay **4/5** · acquisition ease **5/5** · developer fit **5/5** · **total 19/20**.

### 3. Local-LLM hardware fit calculator

- **Positioning:** A cited “will it fit and what will it cost?” planner for local-LLM buyers and operators.
- **Pain and evidence:** Developers cannot reliably map model, quantization, context, and concurrency to their Mac or GPU fleet. [AirLLM](https://github.com/lyogavin/airllm) and [ds4](https://github.com/antirez/ds4) together drew 6,541 weekly stars by attacking this exact constraint; paid report demand is **to be validated**.
- **Target and first 20 prospects:** 20 independent AI agencies and local-inference consultants actively discussing a $2,000–$20,000 workstation/server purchase in LocalLLaMA, Discord, or hardware forums.
- **Product and difference:** Convert model architecture and actual inventory into RAM/VRAM, SSD-streaming, throughput, power, and cost ranges. Unlike static compatibility tables, it compares runtimes on the buyer’s precise context and concurrency assumptions with provenance and confidence bands.
- **Solo leverage:** Public model configs and benchmark submissions support a useful rules engine before a large dataset exists.
- **MVP (1–2 weeks):** (1) hardware form, (2) model/config importer, (3) memory-fit and throughput range, and (4) downloadable comparison for AirLLM, ds4, llama.cpp, ten models, Apple Silicon, and six GPU classes. **Do not build:** a benchmark cloud, hardware marketplace, or purchase recommendation engine.
- **Implementation:** Next.js, Python/FastAPI calculation service, Hugging Face config API, runtime README parsers, SQLite/Postgres, and client-side charts.
- **Revenue and test price:** Free single scenario; $29 cited purchase report; $99/month agency inventory.
- **Acquisition and first outreach:** Answer live model–GPU compatibility questions with a transparent free calculation, then DM: “I modeled your exact context/concurrency; $29 gets the three-runtime comparison and sensitivity ranges.”
- **7-day validation:** Days 1–2 collect 30 recurring questions; day 3 build ten manual reports; day 4 recruit five reviewers; days 5–6 sell the report in relevant threads without spam; day 7 measure error corrections and paid conversions.
- **First paid gate:** Ten $29 reports or one $99 team subscription before adding another runtime.
- **Risk, falsification, and stop:** Kernel/settings variance can destroy trust. Fastest disproof: estimates miss measured memory by over 15% or throughput by over 35% on five tests. Stop if accuracy fails after one correction cycle or fewer than three of 30 qualified users pay.
- **Score:** feasibility **5/5** · willingness to pay **4/5** · acquisition ease **5/5** · developer fit **5/5** · **total 19/20**.

### 4. Agent-memory privacy scrubber

- **Positioning:** A local-first policy gateway for every write to shared AI-agent memory.
- **Pain and evidence:** Shared memory accumulates secrets, personal data, stale instructions, and hard-to-delete records. [TencentDB-Agent-Memory](https://github.com/TencentCloud/TencentDB-Agent-Memory) gained 6,444 weekly stars, proving adoption interest; privacy-gateway willingness to pay remains **to be validated**.
- **Target and first 20 prospects:** Security-minded engineering leads at 20 software teams already running two or more agents against Qdrant, pgvector, Pinecone, or a graph store.
- **Product and difference:** Classify writes, block sensitive fields, attach source/expiry, and verify deletion across stores. Unlike a generic DLP product, it understands memory chunks, embeddings, summaries, parent sources, and cascading deletion.
- **Solo leverage:** A gateway architecture can integrate existing stores without replacing them.
- **MVP (1–2 weeks):** (1) intercept writes from one agent SDK, (2) five PII/secret policies, (3) expiry and source metadata, (4) Qdrant deletion verification, and (5) a signed receipt. **Do not build:** broad enterprise DLP, automated compliance claims, or more than one store adapter.

## Three tempting directions rejected this time

1. **A general-purpose personal AI agent:** agent workspaces and coding tools are hot, but the buyer, urgent job, and distribution wedge are too broad; it would compete on model access and brand rather than a two-week, chargeable workflow.
2. **A humanoid-robot operating platform:** robotics momentum is real, but hardware access, safety testing, long integrations, and enterprise sales violate the cash, time, and solo-operation constraints. Only the software-only supplier-change feed survived.
3. **An AI therapist or automated investment adviser:** current news shows both demand and regulatory pressure, but autonomous clinical or financial judgment creates unacceptable licensing, safety, and liability exposure. The retained ideas only organize evidence for licensed human review.

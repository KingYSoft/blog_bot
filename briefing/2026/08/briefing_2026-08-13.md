## Method

The sole repository inclusion source was the live, official [GitHub Trending](https://github.com/trending) page under its default filters: **global, any spoken language, any programming language, Today/Daily**. The page was read from the Trending heading through the final repository and footer, then independently re-fetched twice. All three reads returned the same 17 repositories in the same order. No cached list, search result, third-party ranking or extra repository was used. Every repository homepage and README was then read, while lifetime stars, primary language, license, archive status, open-issue count and last-push time were cross-checked through the official GitHub API. Lifetime and daily-star figures are snapshots and can move after the stated capture time.

News selection separated event date from publication time, favored first-party releases and public authorities, and used authoritative reporting for market synthesis or where a primary release was unavailable. Because the cutoff is midday in Shanghai but early August 13 in Europe and the Americas, the candidate pool includes late-August 12 events that remain current in Asia. Material figures were checked against a second source where available. Startup concepts were screened from the complete 17-repository list and 20-story news pool, then constrained to one full-stack founder, a 1–2 week MVP, normally under $500 cash cost, direct online distribution and a paid-validation threshold. Trend data is evidence of attention, not proof of willingness to pay.

**Cross-project comparison.** `Orca` and `Paperclip` manage agent work at different layers—developer worktrees versus organizational control—while `Switchyard` chooses models, `Semantica` records why decisions occurred, and `Needle` moves a bounded subset of tool calling onto devices. `diagram-design`, `ppt-master` and `LTX-2` show a parallel shift from generic generation toward editable, verifiable production artifacts. ## 15 startup experiments for a solo software founder

### 1. CI failure-cluster digest for AI-heavy pull requests — **19/20 (5+5+4+5)**

- **Positioning:** A GitHub app that groups noisy CI failures after agent-generated PR bursts and names the one likely root cause.
- **Pain / evidence:** Blacksmith's $45 million round and 5,000-customer claim show validation is becoming a bottleneck; Orca's 1,235 daily stars show parallel agents increase concurrent diffs. Teams currently read long Actions logs or pay for broad CI platforms.
- **Customer / first 20:** 5–25 developer SaaS teams using GitHub Actions plus Copilot/Codex/Claude; find engineering leads posting about flaky tests or >20 PRs/day.

- **Business model:** $29/month for five repositories; first month at $19.

## Three popular-looking directions rejected

1. **Another general agent operating system.** Orca, Paperclip, Macro and the skill ecosystems already span worktrees, organizations, tools and shared memory. One founder cannot match their security, integrations and cross-device surface in two weeks; a prototype would not be a defensible paid wedge.
2. **An autonomous stock-picking or trading bot.** Kronos validates technical interest, not excess returns. Data rights, look-ahead bias, regime change, transaction costs, trust and regulation make direct signals a poor low-risk MVP; a timestamp/regime audit is safer and testable.
3. **A creator “AI rights marketplace.”** Twitch's default creates urgency, but a marketplace needs platforms, creators and buyers simultaneously plus legal clarity. The permission auditor has one buyer, produces immediate evidence and does not wait for network effects.
